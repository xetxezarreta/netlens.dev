01Registry

—

Port assignments do not prove the application. A service can listen on any port, and a capture can show TLS on 8443 or SSH on 2222. The payload and handshake should be examined in addition to the port number.

03Notes

ports are indicators, not proof
  • A port is not a protocol. TLS can run on 443, 8443, 993 or any port. QUIC usually uses UDP 443 but does not have to.
  • Source ports are ephemeral. A client's source port is chosen locally and changes per connection. Only the destination usually identifies a service.
  • Both TCP and UDP can share a number. DNS uses 53 on both; the transport is part of the flow identity.
  • Firewall rules should not rely only on port numbers. Application identification, TLS SNI/ALPN and protocol parsing are more reliable, though each has its own limits.
  • IANA registration is not deployment. A registered port may be unused; an unregistered port may be the de facto standard for a product.
